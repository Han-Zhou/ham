# Zeyuan Allen-Zhu: homepage and the Physics of Language Models

Asked by HanZ on 2026-10-28.

Zeyuan Allen-Zhu's personal homepage is the entry point to his "Physics of Language Models" program. The program runs controlled experiments on small, synthetic setups to find general laws of how LLMs learn knowledge, reason, and generalise. Why it's cool: it asks scientific questions about models instead of chasing benchmarks, and some of its claims have reached practice, such as rewriting pretraining data. The page also lists earlier theory work, including a paper behind LoRA (speculative link, as described by the author).

## Details

- The homepage lists the Physics of LM tutorial at ICML 2024, a DOGE for AI project, a public blog, talks, and publications.
- Part 3.1 of the series (arXiv 2309.14316) argues that rewriting and paraphrasing pretraining data helps models learn knowledge efficiently. The author says this is now standard practice at frontier labs. That is his claim and was not checked here.
- Earlier work covers ensemble and knowledge distillation (arXiv 2012.09816, listed as an ICLR 2023 Best Paper Runner-Up) and a theory paper on why deep networks beat layer-wise training (arXiv 2001.04413).
- The page says that theory line "inspired" LoRA (arXiv 2106.09685). That is the author's account.
- Affiliations listed on the page: Meta FAIR (2022-2026), Microsoft Research Redmond (2017-2022), Princeton/IAS (2015-2017), MIT (PhD, 2010-2015), Tsinghua (2006-2010).

## Why it matters (speculative)

- Controlled, synthetic experiments are a clean way to test claims about model behaviour, and the Physics of LM series is a good example of that style.
- Next step: read Part 3.1 (arXiv 2309.14316) and see how far its paraphrasing result holds on your own data.
